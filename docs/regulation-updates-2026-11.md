# Regulation updates, November 2026

Law as of **20 November 2026**.

The regulation chapters of this site (5, 7, 8, 9, 13) were written in February and March 2026 and first published on 6 March 2026. On 27 July 2026 Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amended the AI Act, and other things moved too. This note records what was checked on 20 November 2026, what was changed on the site, and what was checked and left alone. The reader-facing version is [updates.html](../updates.html).

## How the checking was done

Every change rests on a primary text read on 20 November 2026.

- **AI Act.** Read from the EUR-Lex consolidated text `02024R1689-20260727` and from the Official Journal text of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, article by article.
- **GDPR.** Read from the consolidated text; recital 26 from the Official Journal version (consolidated texts carry no recitals).
- **DORA, Data Act, Data Governance Act, ePrivacy, the Digital Omnibus on data, adequacy decisions, the EU-US framework, the German KI-MIG.** Read from the official pages: EUR-Lex, the Court's register, the Commission, the Council, the Parliament, the European Supervisory Authorities, the EDPB, the Bundesgesetzblatt and the Bundestag. The deciding sentences were read a second time in the downloaded originals before anything was written on the site. Two pages block scripts and were read in a browser: the Court's register entry for C-703/25 P (status "Pending") and the Council press release of 21 October 2026.
- **Links.** Every source URL on the site was fetched on 20 November 2026. All answer; the Council page answers browsers only.

## Adding the next update

1. Edit the chapter. Wrap the changed text in a marker: a `div.dvf-upd` block for a passage, a `span.dvf-upd-i` with `<del>` and `<ins>` for a date or a term. Give the first marker of an update `id="uNN-MM"`; every marker carries `data-upd="uNN-MM"`.
2. Add the entry to `data/updates.json` (what the page said, what it says now, the source and where in the source). Give it a `"date"` if it is not the site's "law as of" date.
3. When the whole site has been re-checked, change `law_as_of` in `data/updates.json`, `LAW_AS_OF` in `nav-inject.js`, the banner date in each chapter and the strip in `index.html`.
4. Run `python3 tools/build_updates.py`. It refuses to pass if a marker and the list disagree.

Before a deploy, run both checks: `python3 tools/build_updates.py --check` (list, markers, banners, navigation and home page agree) and `python3 tools/check_embed_header.py` (the deploy keeps every site that is allowed to frame this one).

After a change to `tools/build_updates.py` itself, run `python3 tools/test_build_updates.py`. Each case changes one thing in a copy of the site and runs the script: most cases break the copy and expect the check to say so, a few change it harmlessly and expect silence.

What the check covers: the list, the markers in the chapters (present, dated and labelled as the list says, not hidden by their own markup, and showing some new or earlier text), the "Updated" links, the banners, the navigation date and the cards on the home page. It reads the pages with Python's HTML reader, so quotes, capitals and comments do not fool it.

What it cannot cover: whether a sentence about the law is right, and whether a "said before" text is what the page really said. A wrong date inside a marker passes. Those two things were checked by reading the sources and the earlier version of each page, and that is what the tables above record.
